Welcome to Jefferson Park

The Start and Growth

Named after President Thomas Jefferson, the Jefferson Township was formed in 1850 and was home to about 50 buildings. The neighborhood is located on the northwest edge of the city of Chicago and is nicknamed the "Gateway to Chicago" as a result of it being a transportation hub. These growths of railways in the area led to an influx of Polish, German, Russian, Italian, Czech, and Slovakian immigrants. In the 1950s, the (now known as) Kennedy Expressway was being constructed and finished in the late 1950s which supplied even more means of transportation in and out of Jefferson park. With the major growth of Polish migrants, by the 1990s nearly half of the population was of Polish descent. In 1970, the Chicago Transit Authority (CTA) built a terminal which supported, and still supports, the CTA Blue Line, Metra Union Pacific Northwest line, and many bus routes. These various transportation methods allow for residents to easily commute to and from downtown Chicago, O'Hare International Airport, and many other locations.
